Ever wondered where the power from those roof panels actually goes? Let's follow it.
Sunlight hits the panels, and each one makes a little direct current. They're wired together, so their power adds up and flows down a single cable.
But your house runs on alternating current, so that cable goes to an inverter, a box that converts direct current into alternating current.
From the inverter, the power goes to your breaker panel, and from there to your lights, your fridge and your air conditioner.
Plug in more things, like a second air conditioner, and if the panels can't keep up, the grid quietly covers the difference. To run it all on sun, you add more panels, and sometimes a bigger inverter, because the inverter limits how much power can flow at once.
At noon you usually make more than you use, so the extra flows out to the grid, and in many places a two-way meter counts it.
At night the panels stop. To keep running on your own power, you need a battery bank that stores the extra from the day. Without one, you pull from the grid.
And during a blackout? A normal grid-tied system shuts itself off, to protect the workers fixing the lines. Only batteries with a hybrid inverter keep your lights on.
